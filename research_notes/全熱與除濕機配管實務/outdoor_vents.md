# ERV outdoor air intake (OA, 新風進氣口) and exhaust (EA, 排氣口) placement on the facade: avoiding short-circuit (短循環/回吸) and contamination

## Q1. Minimum OA–EA separation (manufacturer and code values) and recommended orientation

### Takeaway
Sources disagree on the number. The Mitsubishi Lossnay RX3/RX5 manuals give "1 m or more" for the 15–65 m³/h-class small units (the meaning of that distance is disputed; see Gaps). Delta (台達) Taiwan says at least 1.5 m. North-American ERV makers mostly say 6 ft (1.8 m). ASHRAE 62.2 says 10 ft (3 m), now relaxed to a 5 ft (1.5 m) stretched-string distance for exhausts serving the same dwelling, under conditions. Daikin's small VKM units say only 3× duct diameter (≈0.45 m for Ø150). Orientation rules matter as much as distance: separate faces or wide horizontal spacing, no side-by-side or concentric caps in windy places, never put the inlet directly under or in front of the exhaust jet, and keep the inlet at least 0.6 m sideways from a downward-discharging exhaust.

The RX5 manual gives the same tiers: LGH-15~65RX5 1 m, 80/100 2.5 m, 150/200 3 m. The search summarizer read these as minimum distances between the outdoor air intake and the exhaust. The same table sits next to the instruction "downward duct slope of 1/30 or more toward the wall side, plus a deep hood, to keep rainwater out" — [Lossnay LGH-F300RX5 manual (search excerpt)](https://resource.gemaire.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/mitsubishi_lgh-f300rx5-e_article_1369225213463_en_ii.pdf?fmt=pdf); [Lossnay RX3 manual mirror (search excerpt)](https://www.c-o-k.ru/library/instructions/mitsubishi-electric/ventilyacionnoe-oborudovanie-i-komplektuyuschie/24346/113054.pdf). **Code values**
- **[HARD, ASHRAE 62.2-2013 §6.8]** "Air inlets that are part of the ventilation design shall be located a minimum of 10 ft (3 m) from known sources of contamination such as a stack, vent, exhaust hood, or vehicle exhaust." — [ASHRAE 62.2-2013 Addendum h](https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/Standards%20and%20Guidelines/Standards%20Addenda/62_2_2013_h_20150707.pdf)
- **[HARD, ASHRAE interpretation]** Local bathroom and kitchen exhausts "are also considered known sources of contamination and require a 10-foot separation" — [ASHRAE IC 62.2-2010-4](https://www.ashrae.org/File%20Library/Technical%20Resources/Standards%20and%20Guidelines/Standards%20Intepretations/IC62-2-2010-4.pdf). This conflicts with the IRC, which per GBA does not treat toilet, bath or kitchen exhaust as "hazardous" contaminants under R303.5.1 — [GreenBuildingAdvisor: ERV/HRV exhaust and supply vent hoods](https://greenbuildingadvisor.com/article/erv-hrv-exhaust-and-supply-vent-hoods).
- **[HARD, ASHRAE 62.2-2022 §6.6 + Addendum f (2024)]** In the 2022 edition the clause is §6.6, and the source list adds plumbing vent terminations, combustion vents, exhaust hoods, driveways, streets, alleys, parking lots and loading docks. Conditions: the inlet must be ≥2 ft (0.6 m) horizontally from any exhaust that discharges downward, and it must not sit above a horizontally discharging exhaust in the direction of the inlet. For factory combined intake/exhaust terminations no separation is required, provided exhaust air in the intake airflow is ≤10% as established by the manufacturer (this does not cover kitchen exhaust). The excerpt was partly garbled — [ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Addendum f](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/62_2_2022_f_20240221.pdf); [Addendum d](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/62_2_2022_d_20230630.pdf)

- **[HARD, UK]** Approved Document F 2021 Vol. 1 (Dwellings) took effect 15 June 2022 — [Planning Portal](https://www.planningportal.co.uk/applications/building-control-applications/building-control/approved-documents/part-f-ventilation/approved-document-f-volume-1-applies-to-dwellings). **Orientation rules**
- **[PRACTICE, Japan design guide]** 排気口 must be placed so it does not affect the 外気取入口 or adjacent buildings. 厨房/便所 exhaust should in principle go on a different wall face (別の壁面) from the OA intake; if on the same face, take care that exhaust is not drawn in. Combining supply and exhaust in one tower with only a small height difference lets exhaust mix into supply, so separate towers are needed — [空気調和設備 design guide PDF](https://d345i7cbfi6qy8.cloudfront.net/documents/3253/4kuukichouwa.pdf)
- **[PRACTICE, Japan]** ショートサーキット = supply and exhaust openings too close, so air circulates in a small zone and ventilation efficiency drops sharply. The countermeasure is to separate them as far as possible, e.g. diagonally (対角線) — [FJ-tec 空調用語集: ショートサーキット](https://ac.fj-tec.co.jp/?p=9159)
- **[PRACTICE, GBA]** "Side-by-side or concentric intake/exhaust arrangements will result in some level of recirculation of exhaust, especially in windy conditions, and should be avoided." Separate penetrations are always preferable to combined fittings. Cluster all exhausts on one side so the intake can be as far away as possible; 6 ft minimum between the intake and any exhaust — [GBA Q&A: separate ERV intake and exhaust or tandem](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/separate-erv-intake-and-exhaust-or-tandem); [GBA Q&A: how far apart must my HRV fresh air intake be](https://greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/how-far-apart-must-my-hrv-fresh-air-intake-be-from-exhaust)
- **[PRACTICE, US DOE/PNNL]** Inlets too close to exhaust outlets are a common defect. Benchmark: inlets ≥10 ft stretched-string distance from known contamination sources. Coordinate inlet location with other trades early — [PNNL BASC: Ventilation air inlet locations](https://basc.pnnl.gov/resource-guides/ventilation-air-inlet-locations)

### Inferences
- The LGH-35RVX3-E falls in the smallest tier of the Mitsubishi tables, so if the table means OA–EA spacing, the minimum is **1 m**. Because the meaning is disputed (hood spacing versus wall-to-unit duct length), the safe reading is to meet 1 m as hood spacing **and** keep the 1/30 slope with a deep hood.
- A defensible tiered target for one facade: **absolute floor 1 m** (Mitsubishi small tier) → **≥1.5 m** (Delta; also ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Add. f stretched-string for same-dwelling exhaust) → **≥1.8 m** (Broan/GBA) → **≥3 m** (ASHRAE 62.2 base rule, IRC 10 ft) where the facade width allows. Typical Taiwan townhouse frontages are about 4–6 m wide (my assumption, not sourced), so 2.5–3.5 m horizontal spacing at the two ends of the master-bedroom wall is often physically possible.
- "Stretched-string" distance means the shortest path around corners and projections, not straight-line distance through a wall. A corner between OA and EA (one on the front face, one on a side return) adds path length and breaks the plume.
- On vertical offset, cold-climate rules (IRC: exhaust ≥3 ft *above* intake) assume warm, buoyant exhaust. In the Taiwan cooling season, ERV exhaust leaves the core somewhere between indoor (≈26 °C) and outdoor (≈32–35 °C) temperature. It is slightly cooler and denser than ambient, so it tends to sink a little. Placing EA directly above OA is therefore *not* automatically safe in summer; ASHRAE Add. f also bars an inlet above a horizontally discharging exhaust in its direction. Recommend **horizontal separation first**: OA and EA at similar height, at opposite ends of the facade. If there must be a vertical offset, never put OA directly beneath a downward-facing EA hood (≥0.6 m lateral offset per Add. f), and keep the EA jet pointing away from OA.
- Turn both hood openings downward (standard), or point the EA outlet away from OA if hood choice allows. Do not use a tandem or concentric combined cap on a typhoon-exposed facade unless the maker documents ≤10% re-entrainment.

## Q2. Distances from other pollutant and heat sources (AC outdoor unit, gas water heater flue, range hood, dryer, bath exhaust, street/parking, garbage, neighbours' windows) and from ground, roofs and balconies

### Takeaway
Codes and ERV makers converge on **≥3 m (10 ft)** from combustion flues/vents, kitchen hood exhaust, streets/driveways/parking and plumbing vents. North-American ERV makers use **≥1.8 m (6 ft)** from dryer vents, furnace exhaust, gas meters and garbage bins, and keep hoods **≥457 mm (18 in)** above grade or snow. Taiwan's own gas-water-heater code sets only *safety* clearances (150–600 mm) between a forced-exhaust (FE) 熱水器 flue terminal and openings, much smaller than IAQ practice. I found **no numeric source** for split-AC outdoor units, so treat them by analogy as a heat/dirt plume source and keep OA out of their discharge.

### Inferences
- **Gas water heater (熱水器排煙)**: the Taiwan code's 150–600 mm are CO-safety clearances to *any* opening. For an ERV intake (which runs continuously and feeds bedrooms), use the IAQ value: **≥3 m (ASHRAE "combustion vents" / IRC "vents, chimneys")**, or have OA ≥0.9 m *below* and well to the side of the flue (IRC exception logic). In Taiwan townhouses the FE 熱水器 is usually at the rear 後陽台/工作陽台 (assumption), so putting both ERV hoods on the front facade usually helps. Confirm the heater location on site.
- **Range hood (排油煙)**: ASHRAE 62.1 treats it as Class 2 and 62.2 as a known source → **≥3 m**. Grease and odour plumes also foul the ERV's OA filter and core. Taiwanese kitchens usually vent to the rear (assumption); check the neighbours' front-facing range-hood outlets too (Delta specifically warns about neighbours' 排油煙).
- **Split AC outdoor unit (冷氣室外機)**: in cooling mode it discharges warm air (my reasoning: condenser heat rejection, typically several °C above ambient; magnitude not sourced) plus dust. If OA ingests that plume, the ERV supplies hotter air to the bedroom and the AC load goes up, defeating the ERV. Treat the AC condenser's *discharge face* like an exhaust: keep OA **out of the discharge jet, preferably ≥1.8–3 m away and not directly above the unit** (the warm plume rises). The EA hood should also not blow into the condenser *intake* coil (a minor efficiency effect). The outdoor unit location is unknown; find it before fixing hood positions.
- **Bathroom exhaust / clothes dryer**: bath exhaust is a "known source" under ASHRAE (10 ft; 5 ft stretched-string allowed under 2022 Add. f if same dwelling). Dryer vents: ≥1.8 m (Lennox). In this house, if bath fans vent to the front facade, cluster them with the ERV EA on one end and put OA on the other (GBA "cluster exhausts" rule).
- **Street / parking / scooters (機車)**: ASHRAE/IRC put streets, driveways and parking at 10 ft. A 2F intake on the front facade is typically ≥3 m above the street and 1F parking. Avoid placing OA right above a 1F garage door or scooter parking spot where idling exhaust rises. The IRC "≥3 ft below the source" exception only works for sources *above* the intake; for ground-level traffic, height is the mitigation.
- **Neighbours' windows/openings (row houses)**: IRC exhaust rule → EA ≥0.9 m from property lines and from any operable opening (own or neighbour's windows), and do not aim the EA at a neighbour's window. Keep OA ≥3 m from neighbours' bath and kitchen vents and AC units on the shared facade plane.
- **Ground / roof / balcony**: ≥457 mm above any surface where debris or snow could collect (makers). On a 2F facade above a 1F canopy, roof slab or balcony floor, keep OA ≥0.5 m above that surface (splash, leaves, heat radiated from sun-baked slabs; reasoning). Avoid putting either hood inside a semi-enclosed 陽台/鐵窗/採光罩 pocket. Enclosed recesses trap the exhaust plume and raise re-entrainment (inference from the Japanese guide's "same face/same tower mixes" logic). Taiwan also has a habit of later enclosing balconies, which can turn a good layout into a short-circuit.

## Q3. Wall caps / vent hoods: type, screens, rain/typhoon protection, backdraft dampers, duct slope, condensation, noise

### Takeaway
The hard requirements are: slope both outdoor ducts **down to the outside at ≥1/30** (≈1.9°; Delta says 1–2°), use a **deep hood (深形フード)**, and never use a plain bent cap or round hood where rain hits it directly. Mitsubishi recommends an **electric (motorised) shutter damper in strong-wind areas**, because wind pressure pushes outdoor air through the unit when it is off. Insulate both outdoor-side ducts against condensation. North-American code mesh is **6–13 mm (¼–½ in)**, corrosion-resistant. No authoritative typhoon-specific hood standard was found.

### Cited Findings
- **[HARD, Mitsubishi]** OA and EA ducts must slope "1/30 or more downward to outdoor" (toward the wall side) and use a deep hood to keep rainwater out — [Lossnay RX3/RX5 manual excerpts](https://resource.gemaire.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/mitsubishi_lgh-f300rx5-e_article_1369225213463_en_ii.pdf?fmt=pdf); JP version: "雨水の浸入を防ぐため、ダクト下りこう配1/30以上(壁側へ)" — [Mitsubishi JP 施工説明書](https://dl.mitsubishielectric.co.jp/dl/ldg/wink/ssl/wink_doc/m_contents/wink/FAN_IM/0903873hh8302_im.pdf)
- **[HARD, Mitsubishi – older Lossnay series]** "Do not install the vent cap or round hood where it will come into direct contact with rainwater." — [Lossnay series technical manual (search excerpt)](https://manualslib.com/manual/1571205/Mitsubishi-Electric-Lossnay-Series.html?page=82)
- **[HARD/RECOMMENDED, Mitsubishi]** "In cold area or strong wind area, outdoor air may enter the unit because of the pressure difference or external wind even when the unit stops. - **[HARD, Daikin]** "Do not use a bent cap or a round hood as the outdoor hood if they might get rained on directly." — [Daikin VKM50GMV1 manual](https://manualshive.com/en/manual/daikin/vkm50gmv1/4705921?page=3). "Install the two outdoor ducts with down slope to prevent rainwater from entering the unit." — [Daikin VAM GVJU manual](https://daikinac.com/docs/default-source/energy-recovery-ventilator/im-vam300-1200gvju.pdf). OA, EA and RA ducting needs a minimum length of 1.5 m; if shorter or absent, grilles must be fitted on the openings — [Daikin VAM-J8 manual](https://www.daikin.eu/content/dam/document-library/installation-manuals/vent/heat-reclaim-ventilation/vam-j8/VAM-J8_Installation.Operation%20manual_4PEN664011-1D_English.pdf)
- **[PRACTICE, Delta Taiwan]** Both outdoor-connected ducts slope 1–2° outward against rain ingress and are wrapped with insulation (隔熱材) to prevent condensate (冷凝水). Fit filters at the outdoor end of both OA and EA — [Delta FAQ](https://www.deltaww.com/zh-tw/FAQ/2020). Fit an outdoor hood (外氣罩) or insect screen (防蟲網) at the EA duct end against birds and rain — [Delta VEB500AT manual](https://filecenter.delta-china.com.cn/Products/Download/03/0302/manual/VEB500AT.pdf)
- **[HARD, IRC R303.6]** Outdoor intake and exhaust openings must be protected with corrosion-resistant screens, louvers or grilles with openings "not less than 1/4 inch and a maximum opening size of 1/2 inch" in any dimension. Openings must also be protected against weather — [Everett WA code excerpt (IRC R303.6)](https://lfportal.everettwa.gov/WebLink/0/doc/1853734/Page19.aspx). (Section numbering varies by edition: R303.5 or R303.6.)
- **[PRACTICE, UK example]** A London residential scheme's Part F compliance document specifies bird mesh on both terminals and a 90° cowl on the exhaust — [Camden planning document](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/9059837/file/document)
- **[PRACTICE, product]** Wind-protection inserts exist for exposed coastal/storm locations (Inventer, Swiss) to handle high wind pressure on wall terminals — [Inventer wind protection insert](https://ecologicalbuildingsystems.com/product/inventer-wind-protection-insert)

### Inferences
- **Slope**: 1/30 = 33 mm drop per metre (≈1.9°), consistent with Delta's 1–2°. Ø150 ducts must fall continuously from the unit to the wall sleeve, with no sag or belly where water pools (flexible duct sags easily; rigid or semi-rigid is better near the wall).
- **Hood type for a typhoon facade**: a **deep hood (深形フード) with downward opening**, stainless or ASA/UV-stable resin, sealed to the wall sleeve with flashing/sealant. Prefer hoods with an integrated **gravity backdraft shutter** or fit a **motorised shutter damper** on both OA and EA (Mitsubishi's strong-wind recommendation). This stops typhoon wind pressure from pushing wind-driven rain and air through the stopped unit, and stops stack/wind backflow into the bedroom. In Taiwan, several typhoons per year on an exposed front facade makes this the "strong wind area" case (inference).
- **Insect mesh**: the code ¼–½ in (6–13 mm) range is bird/rodent mesh. Finer mosquito-grade mesh (~1–2 mm) on the hood clogs quickly with dust and gecko or insect debris and raises pressure drop and whistle noise. The LGH unit already has an OA filter. Recommend ~6 mm stainless bird mesh on the hood, plus an *accessible, cleanable* finer insect screen only if the hood is reachable for cleaning from a window (2F facade access is hard).
- **Condensation**: in Taiwan summer the OA duct carries hot, humid outdoor air (dew point often ≥25 °C) through a ceiling cooled by AC to ~26 °C, so condensation forms on the outside of the duct. The EA duct carries cooled exhaust, so its outer surface can sweat in the hot ceiling void. Both need vapour-sealed insulation all the way to the wall sleeve (Delta). Insulating only the "cold" duct is a common mistake (inference).
- **Noise**: at 350 m³/h through Ø150 the face velocity is ≈5.5 m/s (my arithmetic: 350/3600 ÷ 0.0177 m²). Hood louvres or fine mesh at that velocity can whistle, and the EA hood is right outside the master bedroom. Prefer low-resistance deep hoods and avoid fine mesh. Consider a short flexible silencer section near the unit.
- **Separation from wall-cap drips**: an EA hood discharging downward and dripping (condensate or rain) should not sit directly above OA. This repeats ASHRAE Add. f's 2 ft lateral rule.

## Q4. How to verify there is no short-circuit after installation

### Takeaway
I found no ERV-specific commissioning procedure for outdoor re-entrainment. The literature supports **smoke visualisation** (qualitative) and **tracer-gas mass balance** (quantitative; CO2 can serve as the tracer). A simple field version uses temperature or CO2 differences between ambient air, the OA duct and the EA duct to compute a recirculation fraction.

### Cited Findings
- **[PRACTICE]** Tracer-gas methods are used to evaluate re-entrainment of exhaust at building air intakes, and are often the only way to get quantitative airflow data. Smoke release gives visual confirmation of airflow patterns (NIOSH protocol, written for local exhaust, not ERVs) — [CDC stacks (NIOSH)](https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/199419); [AIVC](https://www.aivc.org/node/20315)
- **[PRACTICE]** A residential ERV study injected acetone and isopropanol into the exhaust stream and found up to 17% transfer to the supply (isopropanol, balanced flow). This is *internal* cross-transfer in the exchanger, not facade short-circuit, but it shows a tracer test can detect transfer — [RAJAR paper, doi 10.47191/rajar/v8i5.19](https://doi.org/10.47191/rajar/v8i5.19)
- **[HARD/benchmark]** For factory combined terminations, ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Add. f limits exhaust air in the intake airflow to ≤10% as established by the manufacturer. This is the only numeric acceptance threshold found — [ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Add. f](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/62_2_2022_f_20240221.pdf)
- **[PRACTICE]** UK ADF commissioning record forms (Zehnder) exist for checking installed ventilation, but I did not find their content on terminal re-entrainment — [Zehnder ADF commissioning record](https://products.zehnder.co.uk/fileadmin/user_upload/UK/adf-2010-2021-edition---commissioning-inspection-record.pdf)

### Inferences (proposed field procedure; my own engineering, not from a source)
1. **Visual check / photos**: measure the stretched-string OA–EA distance and the distances to the AC condenser, 熱水器 flue, bath fans and neighbours' vents. Record them against the Q1/Q2 targets.
2. **Smoke test**: with the LGH at its highest fan speed, release smoke (smoke pencil, incense, or a theatrical fog puff) at the EA hood outlet. Watch whether any is drawn into the OA hood. Repeat on a windy day and with the wind blowing along the facade from EA toward OA (the worst case). Also release smoke at the AC condenser discharge and the 熱水器 flue with the ERV running.
3. **Temperature mass balance** (best when indoor–outdoor ΔT ≥ 6–8 °C, e.g. a summer afternoon with AC on, or a cool winter night): measure ambient T_amb (shaded, ≥3 m from all vents), T_EA at the EA hood outlet, and T_OA inside the OA duct just behind the hood. Recirculation fraction R ≈ (T_OA − T_amb)/(T_EA − T_amb). Target R ≈ 0 within sensor error. R > 0.1 suggests a problem (by analogy with the ASHRAE 10% combined-termination limit). Sun-heated hoods and walls bias T_OA upward, so measure on a shaded facade or at night. The same method with the AC condenser running detects condenser-plume ingestion.
4. **CO2 mass balance**: raise indoor CO2 (occupants or a CO2 cylinder) so the exhaust is ≥1,000 ppm above outdoor air. Measure C_amb, C_EA and C_OA (in the OA duct) and compute R = (C_OA − C_amb)/(C_EA − C_amb). With typical NDIR accuracy of ±30–50 ppm, R resolution is only about ±5%, hence the large indoor–outdoor difference needed.
5. **Long-term check**: log indoor CO2 in the master bedroom overnight. A properly working 350 m³/h-class ERV serving a bedroom should hold CO2 well below about 1,000 ppm. If CO2 stays high despite good measured airflow, suspect short-circuit (or internal leakage).

### Gaps
- I found no published ERV/HRV commissioning standard (ASHRAE Guideline 1.1, NEBB/AABC, BSRIA, JIS) giving a facade re-entrainment test or acceptance criterion. Only the 10% combined-termination limit is sourced.
- I found no data on whether the LGH-RVX3's internal temperature sensing could log OA intake temperature to support step 3. This was not checked.

## Applying the rules to this house (Taiwan townhouse, 2F master-bedroom front facade, LGH-35RVX3-E, Ø150)

### Takeaway
On a single front facade, the defensible layout is: **OA and EA at the two far ends of the master-bedroom wall at similar height, ≥1.5 m apart and ideally ≥3 m stretched-string**; both deep hoods opening downward with shutters; EA clustered with any other front-side exhausts; OA away from and upwind of the AC condenser discharge, 熱水器 flue and street plume. The minimum floor is 1 m (Mitsubishi small-unit table), but that number's meaning is unconfirmed.

### Cited Findings
- Mitsubishi small-unit tier: 1 m or more — [Lossnay RX3/RX5 manual excerpts](https://resource.gemaire.com/is/content/Watscocom/Gemaire/mitsubishi_lgh-f300rx5-e_article_1369225213463_en_ii.pdf?fmt=pdf)
- Delta Taiwan: ≥1.5 m, 1–2° outward slope, insulate both outdoor ducts, avoid neighbours' 排油煙 — [Delta FAQ](https://www.deltaww.com/zh-tw/FAQ/2020)
- ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Add. f: 1.5 m stretched-string to same-dwelling exhausts, ≥0.6 m lateral from a downward exhaust, inlet not above a horizontal exhaust aimed at it — [ASHRAE 62.2-2022 Add. f](https://www.ashrae.org/file%20library/technical%20resources/standards%20and%20guidelines/standards%20addenda/62_2_2022_f_20240221.pdf)
- Avoid side-by-side caps, especially in wind — [GBA Q&A](https://www.greenbuildingadvisor.com/question/separate-erv-intake-and-exhaust-or-tandem)
- Electric damper in strong-wind areas — [Lossnay series technical manual](https://manualslib.com/manual/1571205/Mitsubishi-Electric-Lossnay-Series.html?page=82)

### Inferences
- **Priority checklist for the installer (施工確認清單)**:
  1. Find the split-AC outdoor unit, the 熱水器 flue, the range hood and the bath-fan outlets (own and both neighbours') before drilling. Put OA at the end of the facade farthest from all of them, and EA at the end nearest the existing exhausts.
  2. OA–EA horizontal centre spacing: **target ≥3 m; accept ≥1.5 m; never <1 m**. Same height ±0.3 m; never stack OA directly under EA.
  3. Heights: both hoods ≥0.5 m above any slab, canopy or balcony floor below them, and not inside a 鐵窗/採光罩/enclosed-balcony pocket. EA ≥0.9 m from any openable window (own or neighbour's), and not blowing at a neighbour's window.
  4. Deep hoods (深形), downward-opening, with gravity or motorised shutters and ~6 mm stainless bird mesh. No round caps or bent pipes exposed to direct rain.
  5. Ducts fall ≥1/30 toward the outside all the way; both OA and EA insulated with a vapour barrier up to the wall sleeve; wall sleeve sealed against wind-driven rain.
  6. Commission with the smoke and temperature/CO2 checks from Q4. Re-check if the AC outdoor unit is later moved onto this facade or the balcony is enclosed.
- If the facade is too narrow for ≥1.5 m, use a corner. Put EA on a side return wall or the roof (stretched-string distance grows and the plume is broken by the corner), or put EA on the rear facade. Mitsubishi and the Japanese design guide both favour different faces. A combined tandem cap is a last resort and only with documented ≤10% re-entrainment.
- Prevailing winds in Taiwan (NE monsoon in winter, SW in summer, typhoon winds from varying directions) are my general knowledge and not sourced here. Where a dominant wind runs along the facade, put OA on the upwind end and EA on the downwind end.

### Gaps
- The house-specific layout depends on the unknown AC outdoor-unit location, the facade width, and the 熱水器 and range-hood locations. These are site facts to collect.
- The LGH-RVX3-E manual text on hood spacing and hood accessories still needs a direct read (blocked in this session).
